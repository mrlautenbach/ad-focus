Finding your way

The sidebar, search and shortcuts that get you anywhere in AD Focus.

The sidebar, search and shortcuts

The sidebar groups every screen into six areas: Planning, Athletics, Operations, People, Communication and Admin. Some screens sit under another one; click the arrow beside a row to show them.

If a screen you expect is missing, your school may have switched that module off, or your role may not include it. Your AD can check Settings › School › Your department and Settings › Team › Feature Access….

ShortcutDoes
⌘KCommand palette: type to jump to any screen, record or action
⇧⌘MAll Modules: every screen as a tile with a one-line description
⌥⌘NQuick Add Task, from anywhere in the app
⌘1 – ⌘9Jump to the first nine screens
⌘Z / ⇧⌘ZUndo or redo your own last saves
⇧⌘HEvent Hub for the selected event

Good to know

  • Nothing is sent for you. Notices, emails and calendar changes open as drafts or previews that you confirm.
  • AI only runs when you click, and hides student names by default.
  • When your school is connected, teammates' changes appear within about a second, and their initials show on records they have open.
  • Rearrange the sidebar in Settings › Appearance; Use Recommended Layout puts it back.

Planning

Planning is where your own day and week live: the morning brief, your to-dos, your calendar, your goals and the department's strategic plan.

Today

Find it: Sidebar › Planning › Today (or ⌘1, or ⌘K and type “Today”).

Today is your start screen. It shows the day's calendar, the morning brief, today's games, what is overdue or due, and anything other modules suggest you follow up.

Who uses it: Everyone. Each role gets its own layout, and coaches see a My teams card for the teams they coach.

Common tasks

  1. Read the morning brief. The Morning brief tile gives the day in one line, then one line per module that needs action, such as officials, transport, clearances or budget. Click a line to open the screen where you fix it.
  2. Quiet a line you don't need. Right-click a brief line and choose Hide until tomorrow, Hide for a week or Never mark urgent. Click Show all to bring hidden lines back.
  3. Get a short summary. Click Summarise under the brief. AD Focus shows exactly what will be read, with student names hidden, and runs only on this Mac after you click Send.
  4. Wrap up the day. An hour before your work day ends, the tile becomes End of day. Click Sweep… to see what is still open, then Move … to Tomorrow for unfinished to-dos, or Plan tomorrow to open the calendar.
  5. Review suggested follow-ups. When something changes, such as a moved game, Today lists Suggestions for officials, buses, venue bookings and calendar copies. Click one to preview it, then Apply or Dismiss. Nothing changes until you confirm.
  6. Arrange the screen. Click Customize Today to reorder or hide tiles. The AD can set a layout for each role with Layout for.

Good to know

  • The brief builds overnight, from 04:30 while AD Focus is running, otherwise when you open it. A notification arrives at 07:15 on school days, with a button to open the top line or Snooze 1 hour.
  • Settings › Notifications › Morning brief lets you open AD Focus at login and switch on Summarise the brief overnight on this Mac. It is off by default and uses the on-device model only.
  • If you set up the school, a Get started card lists your next setup steps. Use Mark Done, skip a step, or Hide the card. The full list stays in Settings › Get Started.
  • Coaches: the My teams card shows each team's next game and what needs attention, with a Team Hub button.
  • Type in the capture bar to add a to-do for today, for example “Call bus company @Sam due:fri ~15m”.
  • On iPhone, Today shows the brief and your follow-ups, and a Brief widget is available.

Tasks

Find it: Sidebar › Planning › Tasks (or ⌘K and type “Tasks”).

Tasks is your to-do list. It sorts work into lists, projects and areas, and links to-dos to goals, events and calendar blocks.

Who uses it: AD, Assistant AD, coaches and staff by default. Each person has their own list.

Common tasks

  1. Add a to-do from anywhere. Press ⌥⌘N (Quick Add Task…), type, and press Return. New to-dos go to your Inbox. You can add “@person”, “#domain”, “!goal”, “due:fri” or “~45m”.
  2. Work through your lists. Use Inbox, Today, Upcoming, Anytime, Someday and Logbook in the left column. Drag a to-do onto a list, project or area to file it.
  3. Organise bigger jobs. Click New project or area at the bottom of the list column. Inside a project, add headings to split the work.
  4. Schedule or move a to-do. Right-click a to-do for Due, Goal, Event, Move to Block, Add to Top or Convert to Project.
  5. Paste a list. Copy lines from an email or document and press ⇧⌘V. Each line becomes a to-do.
  6. Run a focus session. Press ⌥⌘F (Start Focus Session…), say what will be done, choose a length and click Start. Or click the play button on a to-do for a Pomodoro. A bar at the top shows the time, with Pause and Finish.

Good to know

  • To sync with another to-do app, go to Settings › Integrations › Task App › Sync tasks with and choose Things 3, Apple Reminders or Google Tasks. AD Focus works fully without one.
  • Reminders and Google Tasks sync both ways: completing a to-do on either side completes both. Things to-dos are read in, and you can send to-dos to Things.
  • Your Top list on Today holds the few to-dos that matter most each day. Set how many in Settings › Planning.
  • Log an interruption during a focus session from the Focus menu › Log Interruption.
  • On iPhone you can see and tick off to-dos, and a Tasks widget is available.

Calendar

Find it: Sidebar › Planning › Calendar (or ⌘2, or ⌘K and type “Calendar”).

Calendar shows your events, games and linked Google and Mac calendars on one time grid. You also block time here for focused work.

Who uses it: AD, Assistant AD, coaches and staff by default.

Common tasks

  1. Change the view. Use Day, 3 Days, Work Week, Week or Month at the top, or press D, 3, 5, W or M. Click Month on the left of the toolbar to jump to a date.
  2. Choose what shows. Use the layers menu to switch My Events, Other Teams' Events, Practices, Blocks and Calendar Entries on or off, or pick one team.
  3. Add an event by typing. Click New Event and type into Quick add, for example “Coaches meeting Tue 3pm for 45m at Gym”. Choose Athletics Event… from its menu for a game or practice.
  4. Move a game. Drag it to a new time. A tag shows any conflicts as you drag. When you drop it, a review sheet shows conflicts and drafts notices. Click Move Event to confirm. Notices are saved as drafts, not sent.
  5. Block time. Drag down on empty time, or click New Block. Drop to-dos onto a block to schedule them. Use Plan › Protect Strategic Time… or Plan Your Day… to find room.
  6. Sort out double entries. When a calendar entry looks like an AD Focus event, click Same Event?…. Choose Same Event — Link Them, Same Event — Hide Calendar Copy or Different Events — Show Both.

Good to know

  • Link Google and Mac calendars in Settings › Calendars. AD Focus only writes to a Google calendar when you switch on Publish AD Focus events here, and you preview every change first.
  • Select an event and click Event Hub… (⇧⌘H) to see its follow-ups, weather, officials, trip, venue, to-dos, checklist, budget and incidents in one sheet.
  • Keyboard: arrow keys step through items; ⌥ + arrows nudge a selected block; ⌘D duplicates it; Delete removes it; ⌥⌘= and ⌥⌘− zoom the hours. Edit › Undo reverses a change.
  • All-day entries such as holidays sit in a strip above the hours. They never count as busy time.
  • For every team's games in one list, use Athletics › Schedule, which also has a Grid view.
  • On iPhone, Calendar shows a week strip above a scrolling agenda, and you can add events by typing.

Goals

Find it: Sidebar › Planning › Goals (or ⌘K and type “Goals”).

Goals holds your personal and department goals for the season or year. AD Focus protects time for each goal every week and tracks progress from the work linked to it.

Who uses it: AD, Assistant AD, coaches and staff by default. A school can switch Goals off in Settings › School › Your department.

Common tasks

  1. Add a goal. Click New Goal and choose New Personal Goal (⌘N) or New Department Goal (⇧⌘N). ⌥Space also opens a quick field.
  2. Describe it. Fill in Why it matters, Measurable outcome, a weekly time target and a “When ___, I will ___” plan. Add an optional metric with baseline, current and target.
  3. Share a department goal. Under Visible to, pick Department, then share with everyone or with selected people.
  4. Link work to it. Right-click a goal for New Project in … or New To-Do in …, or drag to-dos onto it. Tag an event from its inspector with Supports goal.
  5. Check progress. Each goal shows a status such as On track or Off track, and a six-week strip. Linked work counts finished to-dos, milestones and events held.

Good to know

  • Use Show to filter goals, and Archived to see old ones.
  • Deleting a goal keeps its to-dos and blocks; they just lose the goal link.
  • A goal shared with you can only be changed by its owner and the AD.
  • Calendar › Plan › Protect Strategic Time… suggests blocks for goals that are behind on time this week.

Strategic Plan

Find it: Sidebar › Planning › Strategic Plan (or ⌘K and type “Strategic Plan”).

Strategic Plan guides you through writing a multi-year plan for your athletics program, one question-led step at a time. It ends with a one-page plan and priorities you can turn into goals.

Who uses it: AD and Assistant AD by default.

Common tasks

  1. Start a plan. Click Start a strategic plan. First add your school's mission, vision and values under School foundations, so the plan is written to serve them.
  2. Work through the steps. The steps are Purpose & mission, Vision, Core values, Current reality, Stakeholders, Strategic priorities, Resources & trade-offs, and Rollout & review. Each has questions and best practice.
  3. Lock in agreed wording. If the department has already adopted a mission or values, use Already adopted? Lock it in. The rest of the plan is checked against it.
  4. Set three to five priorities. Click Add priority, give each a baseline, target and first actions. Click Make it a goal to create a department goal plus an Inbox to-do for each first action.
  5. Check and share it. Open Cohesion check for gaps and a readiness score. Open One-page plan to copy or save it.

Good to know

  • Set how often to review progress in Rollout & review, with Review progress and Next review.
  • Ask AI coach gives a second opinion on how the plan hangs together. It needs a cloud AI key in Settings › AI, and shows exactly what is sent first.
  • A school can switch the plan off with Goals in Settings › School › Your department.

Athletics

Everything about your teams and their competitions: the fixture and practice schedule, game-day running, teams and outside groups, season set-up, leagues, tournaments you host, and trips.

Schedule

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Schedule (or press ⌘K and type “Schedule”).

Every game, practice, meeting and tournament for your teams, as a list or a week grid. It flags clashes for venues, coaches, buses, athletes and exam dates as you plan.

Who uses it: Everyone. ADs and assistant ADs build the schedule; coaches and read-only staff mostly look at it.

Common tasks

  1. Add a game or event. Choose Add › New Event… (⌘N). Set the team, type, home or away, venue and times, then Save. If it clashes, the button reads Save anyway; you are warned, never blocked.
  2. Set up a practice series. Choose Add › New Practice Series…, pick the days, times, venue and first and last day. Turn on Different times or spaces on different days if they vary. To drop one day, select it and choose Skip this day.
  3. Import a league schedule. Choose Add › Import League Schedule… and follow the steps. You can also type an event in plain words with New Event from Text….
  4. Hold a venue provisionally. For a home event with a venue, set Booking to provisional and choose when the Hold expires. Once the hold expires the slot shows as free. Use Confirm booking (⇧⌘K) to firm it up.
  5. Move or change an event. Right-click it and choose Move…, or select it and choose Edit… (⌘E). For weather or other trouble, choose Disruption… to delay, postpone, cancel or reschedule; notices to families and staff are saved as drafts, never sent.
  6. Publish a schedule website. Choose Add › Publish Schedule Pages…. AD Focus writes a folder of read-only pages and calendar feeds for each team, which you upload to your school website. Re-publish whenever the schedule changes.

Good to know

  • Switch between List and Grid in the toolbar. Use the range menu, the Team filter and the Practices toggle to narrow the view.
  • For practices, the Visited box (⇧⌘V) records that you dropped in, who visited and when. It works for past practices too.
  • Event Hub… (⇧⌘H) gathers everything about one event in a single sheet: officials, transport, venue, roles, to-dos and more.
  • Published schedule pages hold no student data: no rosters, names or photos.
  • On iPhone, school events appear in the Calendar tab.

Year

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Schedule › Year (or ⌘K, “Year”).

The whole school year on one screen as twelve small months, with a month view to drill in. Use it to plan the big picture and spot crowded weeks.

Who uses it: Mainly the AD and assistant AD; anyone who can see the schedule can view it.

Common tasks

  1. Switch views. Use the Year, Month and Venues buttons, or ⌃⌘1 for year and ⌃⌘2 for month. ⌘← and ⌘→ step back and forward; Today (⌘T) jumps to now.
  2. Add an event. Choose Add › New Event… (⌘N), or right-click a day and choose New Event on This Day…. The editor has All day and Category.
  3. Import events from a spreadsheet. Choose Add › Import CSV… (⌘I), or drop a CSV file on the window. Match the columns, check the preview, then import. Problem rows and events already in AD Focus are skipped. Save CSV Template… gives you a ready-made file.
  4. Undo an import. Choose Add › Undo Import and pick the file. Its events are removed in one step.
  5. Colour-code events. Choose Add › Categories… to name categories and pick colours. Filter what shows with the Categories and Sources menus.
  6. Print or share. Print… (⌘P) or Export PDF… saves one landscape page.

Good to know

  • Arrow keys move the outlined day. Return opens it; Esc goes from month back to year.
  • Events crossing a month or year boundary appear on both sides.
  • All-day events follow the school time zone (Settings › Planning › Calendar).

Game Day

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Game Day (or ⌘K, “Game Day”).

One card per game on the chosen day, showing roles, supervision, prep to-dos and the people involved. It is your run sheet for the day.

Who uses it: The AD and assistant AD on event days; staff sign up for game-day roles here.

Common tasks

  1. Pick the day. Use Previous day, Today and Next day in the toolbar.
  2. Staff a game. On a card, choose Set up roster from template to add the usual roles (gate, clock, supervision). Staff choose Sign up or Withdraw. More › Unfilled This Week… shows open roles and can create tasks for them.
  3. Work through prep. Each card lists game-day to-dos from a template. Choose Use template to apply one, or edit the items for this game. Manage templates under More › Game-Day Templates….
  4. Handle weather or delays. Choose Disruption… in the toolbar, or Delay / Cancel / Reschedule… on a card. Pick what happened, tick the affected events and choose Apply changes & save drafts. Notices are saved as drafts; nothing is sent. Past changes are in More › Disruption Log….
  5. Prepare the announcer. On a competition card choose PA Script…. The script fills in roster, officials, sponsors and venue details. Set your school details in PA Setup…, add pronunciations, and open Podium for a large-text reading view.

Good to know

  • More › Pop Out to Second Monitor puts Game Day in its own window.
  • Safety, weather and emergency PA wording is default text. Check it against your emergency plan and mark it reviewed in PA Setup.
  • PA scripts and pronunciations stay on your Mac. Polish with AI… runs only when you click it, and names are hidden from the AI.
  • Students who opted out of the directory have their details left out of senior-night reads.

Teams

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Teams (or ⌘K, “Teams”).

Each team’s coaches, practices, upcoming events, roster, costs and surveys in one place. A second tab, Outside groups, holds clubs and rentals that use your facilities.

Who uses it: ADs and assistant ADs set teams up; coaches keep their own rosters current.

Common tasks

  1. Create a team. Choose New Team, pick the sport, level, gender and season, then Create.
  2. Add a roster. Open the team and choose Paste roster… (one name per line) or Import roster file…. For a whole season at once, use Import Season Roster in the toolbar.
  3. Set practices and events. In the team, use Add practice series… and Add event…. They appear in Schedule.
  4. Add an outside group. Switch to Outside groups and choose New Outside Group. Record its organisation, ages, setup needs, agreement and insurance, and fees.
  5. Handle a space request. In the group, choose New request…. The request shows clashes with school teams. Approve creates the group’s sessions; Decline… keeps a reason. Draft reply… prepares a message for you to send.
  6. Keep an outside group informed. Use Draft notice to the group… for changes or cancellations, and Export schedule (.ics)… to send them their sessions as a calendar file.

Good to know

  • School teams come first. An outside group’s page lists clashes with school teams for the next eight weeks.
  • Outside group sessions book venues like practices, so Facilities flags their conflicts.
  • For outside group people you record yes/no clearance, orientation, emergency briefing and key or access flags with dates, never documents.
  • Fees can be counted with Post to Budget income….
  • With no group picked, the Outside groups tab shows usage and estimated fees; Export CSV… saves it.
  • A school can switch Outside groups off in Settings › School › Your department.
  • On iPhone, team rosters are under More › People.

Seasons

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Season Setup › Seasons (or ⌘K, “Seasons”).

Your school years, the seasons inside them, and exam or blackout dates. It is also where you start a season and close one out.

Who uses it: The AD and assistant AD.

Common tasks

  1. Create a school year. Choose New School Year…, set the dates, and add seasons with Add season. Drag seasons to reorder them.
  2. Add exam or blackout dates. Choose Add exam / blackout dates. Events on those dates are flagged: exams as errors, other blackouts as warnings.
  3. Start a season. Choose Start-of-season setup… on a season. Copy teams from a past season, carry over coaches, budget lines and practice schedules, check coach readiness, and create kickoff to-dos.
  4. Check the runway. Choose Season Runway… to see what is ready: games scheduled, coaches assigned and certified, rosters entered, athletes cleared, officials and transport booked, facilities inspected and budgets set. Items the app cannot check use Mark Done.
  5. Close out and archive. Near the end, the same list becomes a close-out: equipment returned, coach evaluations, season costs, awards and the season report. Then choose Archive… and Archive Season.

Good to know

  • A Season runway card appears on Today from six weeks before a season starts; it turns into a close-out card two weeks before the end.
  • When you archive a season you may be offered Move to a New Year or Just Archive. Moving to a new year walks you through what to keep, change or leave behind: teams, coaches, rosters (graduates drop, grades go up), budget, practices and events. Expenses, clearances, trips, incidents, tournaments and equipment are never carried over.
  • Season names and rollover are set in Settings › Seasons.

Leagues

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Season Setup › Leagues (or ⌘K, “Leagues”).

Everything filed under each league or conference you play in: its teams, upcoming games, results, trips, to-dos and budget lines.

Who uses it: The AD and assistant AD, especially before league meetings.

Common tasks

  1. Add your leagues. In Settings › School › Leagues, choose Add league and enter its name, short name and website. If teams already have league names typed in, Create from team entries builds the list for you.
  2. Link teams. In Settings › School › Team leagues, pick a league for each current team. You can also set it on the Teams screen.
  3. Review a league. Pick a league at the top, or All. The board shows totals, then teams, upcoming events, results, trips, to-dos and budget lines. Click any row to open it.
  4. File anything else. Type the league’s short name as a tag (for example #msc) on a to-do or note to file it under that league.

Good to know

  • Games, trips, budget lines and to-dos linked to a league’s teams are filed under it automatically.
  • Each league has a notes field for contacts, meeting dates and rules.
  • League schedules are imported in Schedule › Add › Import League Schedule….

Tournaments

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Season Setup › Tournaments (or ⌘K, “Tournaments”).

Plan a tournament you host: visiting schools, venues, pools and the draw, lodging and transport, catering, volunteers and awards.

Who uses it: The AD, the tournament’s lead and staff given tournament lead responsibility can edit. Others with access see it as view only.

Common tasks

  1. Create a tournament. Choose New Tournament. The setup walkthrough guides you; reopen it from the More menu with Setup Walkthrough…. League Presets stores rules you reuse.
  2. Add teams and venues. In Teams, use Add Team or Import CSV…. In Venues, use Add Venue and set Opening Hours….
  3. Build the draw. In Pools, use Suggest Pools or Add Pool. In Schedule, choose Generate › All Pools, then drag games onto courts. Add Game adds finals. Conflicts show as errors and warnings. Sync to Calendar… puts games on your schedule.
  4. Arrange logistics. The Logistics tab covers lodging, homestay families, transport legs and drivers. Send each school its Visiting Team Packet… from the Teams tab.
  5. Plan meals. In Catering, each group declares how many people need each meal type: no names. The Matrix shows counts and flags. Set up Meal Sessions, then in Caterers & Orders choose Prepare Orders and Email Draft…. At meals, Cards & Serve scans anonymous meal cards.
  6. Staff it. In Volunteers, use Add Shift and Invite; Create Tasks for Unfilled Shifts… follows up gaps. In Awards, Add Standard Awards fills the usual list.

Good to know

  • For individual-sport events, Teams becomes Schools, Schedule becomes Sessions, and there are no pools.
  • The Catering tab appears only when your school has switched catering on. Dietary needs use picklists only and are never sent to AI.
  • Meal orders, procedures and notices are drafts: you send them and then choose Mark Sent….
  • A meal tied to a game is flagged if the game moves or overruns, and Email Timing Change… drafts a note to the caterer.
  • Return to the list with All Tournaments (⌘[).

Trips

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Trips (or ⌘K, “Trips”).

Every away trip at a glance: dates, travelers, linked events, a countdown checklist, budget and the emergency plan.

Who uses it: ADs, assistant ADs and trip leads.

Common tasks

  1. Create a trip. Choose New Trip and pick local, domestic or international. From an event, New Trip for This Event starts one already linked.
  2. Link events. In the trip, use Link other event… or Link all for suggested matches. New event for this trip… adds one.
  3. Add travelers. Choose Add [team] roster. If some athletes are not cleared, pick Add Cleared Only or Add All. Tick Permission, Document and Medical as forms come in.
  4. Work the countdown. The Countdown section lists dated steps for the trip type. Add the standard items with the add-checklist button; overdue items are marked.
  5. Assess and plan. Risk Assessment… opens the trip’s risk assessment and Trip Plan… opens its travel plan.

Good to know

  • Only names, grades and yes/no flags are stored for travelers. Never enter passport or ID numbers.
  • Documents should be valid six months past the return date; the trip warns you when they are not.
  • Skipped trip-plan steps show as open tasks on the trip.

Trip Risk

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Trips › Trip Risk (or ⌘K, “Trip Risk”), or Risk Assessment… on a trip.

A guided, block-by-block risk assessment for each trip, with a risk register, sign-off by an approver and a PDF for your records.

Who uses it: Trip leads fill it in. The trip lead, its creator, the AD and assistant AD can edit. The approver (or any AD when none is set) signs off. Settings are for the AD and assistant AD.

Common tasks

  1. Start an assessment. Choose New Assessment…, pick the trip and template, then Start Assessment. To save time, choose Duplicate a Previous Trip Instead…; every block must still be completed again.
  2. Work through blocks. Answer each block and choose Complete Block, then Next Block. Later blocks stay locked until earlier ones are complete. Use Not applicable where a question does not apply.
  3. Score risks. In the register, confirm the suggested risks or use Add Risks…. Rate likelihood and outcome, choose response measures, and record who was consulted for high scores.
  4. Sign off. Complete every block, type your name, and choose Submit to the approver. While it waits you can Withdraw to Make Changes. The approver chooses Approve, Request changes or Decline.
  5. Reopen after approval. An approved assessment is read-only. Choose Reopen… and give a reason; this voids the approval and it needs re-approval. Who reopened it, when and why is kept.
  6. Follow through. Generate Tasks and Calendar Items… creates to-dos and events from your answers. Export PDF… saves a versioned report.

Good to know

  • When someone else is editing a block, you can read it; it opens for you when they leave.
  • Your school’s approver, scoring scale and default template are in Trip Risk Settings. Manage Options… curates the answer lists.
  • No student medical details or document numbers are recorded: questions ask who holds them, not what they are.

Trip Planner

Find it: Sidebar › Athletics › Trips › Trip Planner (or ⌘K, “Trip Planner”), or Trip Plan… on a trip.

A step-by-step travel plan for a trip: the local cultural and religious calendar, flights and airport transfers, health and evacuation contacts, and visas.

Who uses it: ADs and trip leads planning domestic and international travel.

Common tasks

  1. Create a plan. Choose New Plan…, link it to a trip, then Create Plan. Add people with Add Traveler if they are not on the trip.
  2. Follow the walkthrough. Choose Start walkthrough to go through each section in order. You can only move on when a step is complete or skipped with a reason; a skipped step becomes a task.
  3. Check the calendar. The cultural and religious calendar lists observances at the destination during your dates and any clashes with your events. Add to task list turns one into a to-do.
  4. Enter flights and transfers. Use Add Flight… for each leg and Save Transfer for airport pickups.
  5. Record health contacts. Add hospitals and other contacts with Add… and note when they were last verified. Open Emergency Plans links to your EAPs.
  6. Track visas. Choose the trip purpose, then track each traveler’s visa status and apply-by date. Name who holds the passports and visas.

Good to know

  • AD Focus stores yes/no clearance only, and never passport or visa numbers or copies.
  • Always verify visa rules with the embassy or consulate. The planner is not legal or immigration advice.
  • Live flight status and automatic health and visa briefings work only when your school has them set up.

Operations

The running of your spaces, kit and money: venue bookings and safety checks, equipment, emergency action plans and the department budget.

Facilities

Find it: Sidebar › Operations › Facilities (or press ⌘K and type “Facilities”).

One place for every gym, court, field and room: who has it booked, when it is closed, and where bookings clash. Clashes are flagged, never blocked, so you decide what to move.

Who uses it: The AD, Assistant ADs and facilities managers add and edit bookings. Coaches can book practices and games for their own teams.

Common tasks

  1. See the day or week at a glance. Pick Timeline from the view menu. Use Today (⌘T) and the arrows (⌘[ and ⌘]) to move through dates. Switch to Calendar for one facility by day, week or month.
  2. Book a space. Click Add Booking…, choose the facility, type, team and times, then Save. Games and practices on your schedule with a venue appear here automatically; edit those on the event itself.
  3. Close a space for maintenance or a holiday. Click Add Closure or Maintenance…. Tick Every facility or pick several, and set it to repeat if needed.
  4. Find a free slot. Choose Open Space, set how long you need and the window to search. Click a free time to book it.
  5. Deal with a clash. Clashing bookings are outlined as a Conflict. Each one also creates a to-do for the AD and facilities staff; Open To-Do takes you there, and it ticks itself off once the clash is gone.
  6. Set up a venue. In Venues, click New Venue (⌘N). Set how many groups it can hold at once, its open hours (Set open hours), and any spaces that overlap it.

Good to know

  • Bookings outside a venue’s open hours, during a closure, or in a linked space are flagged too. Times use your school’s time zone.
  • On a home game or practice with a venue, you can set the booking to provisional with a Hold expires time. A lapsed hold frees the slot; Confirm booking (⇧⌘K) books it properly.
  • Agenda gives a printable list (Print…, ⌘P).
  • Each venue has a Venue Hub (Open Venue Hub) with its inspection, this week’s use, clashes and emergency plan.
  • Today shows a Facilities card with open clashes, today’s closures and what is on now and next.
  • When your school has it set up, bookings from Google Calendar facility calendars appear here, read-only.

  4. Write a season review. Click Season Review, choose the season and add your notes. The report compares the season with the one before. Use Copy or Save as Markdown… to keep it.
  5. Draft a narrative with AI. In the season review, click Draft narrative with AI…. You see the exact text first. Coach evaluation scores are left out unless you tick Include coach evaluation scores (this request). Click Send to go ahead.
  6. Check survey results. If your school uses surveys, the Surveys button opens a side panel next to the charts.

Good to know

  • A chart with no data yet goes into a Not started yet tile with a hint, such as “Add rosters to teams.” Add the data and the chart appears.
  • The time and workload charts come from the blocks you plan in Week. The interruptions chart comes from interruptions you log with ⌃⌥I.

Data Health

Find it: Sidebar › Admin › Data Health (or press ⌘K and type “Data Health”).

A checklist of gaps in your setup, such as a team with no coach or a home game with no officials. Each gap lists the records affected, and one click takes you to where you fix it.

Who uses it: Anyone. Each check only covers what your role can see.

Common tasks

  1. See how much needs fixing. The top tiles show Setup gaps (records to fix) and Checks failing. Gaps are grouped under Teams, Schedule, Budget, People, Facilities and Calendars.
  2. Fix a record. Click a record under a check. It opens the right screen, such as the team, the game, the venue or the person, so you can fill in what is missing.
  3. See every record in a check. Each check shows the first three records. Click Show all, or click the check’s title, to see the full list.
  4. Check again after fixing. Click Recheck in the toolbar, or press ⌘R. The page also updates as you change data.

Good to know

  • The checks look for: teams with no coach this year or no season; upcoming games with no venue; home games with no officials; away games with no trip or transport; cost categories no budget rule or line catches; teams with expired or missing clearance flags; coaches with expired certifications; venues with no emergency plan; and game or practice calendars linked to no team.
  • Clearance gaps show as a count per team only, with no student names or reasons.
  • If your role cannot see an area, its check is listed as Not checked here, not shown as passing.
  • When there are gaps, your morning brief shows a short “Setup gaps” line.

AD Brain

Find it: Sidebar › Admin › AD Brain (or press ⌘K and type its name). The screen can be renamed: right-click it in the sidebar and choose Rename…. At a connected school, renaming is up to your AD.

Your own notebook for ideas, meeting notes and things to remember. Write first and file later, link notes to each other and to people, teams and venues, and let older notes come back to you.

Who uses it: Mainly the AD and assistant ADs. Notes are Private to you unless you choose Shared with the school. AD Brain is an add-on some schools have.

Common tasks

  1. Start a note. Click New Note or press ⌥⌘N, then just type. Notes save as you type; ⌘S saves straight away. Type / for block types, callouts and templates such as Meeting, Game debrief and Facility issue.
  2. Use today’s note. Click Today or press ⌥⌘T. It lists the day’s schedule. A Look again card brings back an older note; Another skips it.
  3. Link and mention. Type [[ to link another note, or create a new one. Type @ to mention a person, team, venue, upcoming event or key result. That note then appears in the Notes section on their screen. Press ⌥⌘L for links to and from a note.
  4. Empty the Inbox. Notes with no tag, link, mention or category wait in the Inbox. Click Process Inbox to go through them one at a time: give each a title, file it, then Keep, Merge into… or move it to the Trash.
  5. Find a note. Press ⌘O and type a title, or use Search notes. By… narrows the list by tag, mention, category, kind, recent edits or open to-dos. Save View… keeps a filter in the list.
  6. Ask AI about a note. Click AI and choose Summarize or Extract Action Items. You see the masked text before anything is sent. A to-do can go to your task list with Send to Tasks.

Good to know

  • Obsidian: Settings › Integrations › Notes Apps › Choose Vault Folder… keeps your notes as Markdown files in an Obsidian vault, and edits made there come back. Sync Now runs a sync straight away.
  • Craft: in the same place, connect Craft, then use Import from Craft… to bring documents in as private notes. Send to Craft on a note adds it to a Craft document you choose; you see the masked text first.
  • Similar notes are found on your Mac with Apple’s built-in language model. No note text is sent anywhere for this. It works best with English notes.
  • Pin as Hub keeps a note at the top of your list on this Mac. The OKRs view shows objectives and which notes support each key result.
  • On iPhone, More › Quick Notes lets you capture a note on the go; it lands in your Inbox.

Assistant

Find it: Sidebar › Admin › Assistant (or press ⌘K and type “Assistant”).

AI help with drafting and planning: a morning brief, a draft plan for your week, a weekly review, a chat, and a tone check for emails. Nothing runs or sends on its own. AI only runs when you click.

Who uses it: Whoever the AD gives access to in Settings › Team › Feature Access…

Common tasks

  1. Set up AI. Open Settings › AI and choose a provider: Claude (Anthropic), ChatGPT (OpenAI), Gemini (Google) or another compatible one. Paste your key and click Save key. On a Mac with Apple Intelligence, turn on Use on-device for quick jobs to run briefs, drafts and chat on your Mac with no key.
  2. Get a morning brief. On the Brief tab, click Generate morning brief.
  3. Draft your week. On Draft my week, add anything to consider and click Draft my week. Tick the changes you want, then click the Apply button (it shows how many changes). Nothing changes until you do.
  4. Review your week. On Review, click Review this week. For each suggestion, click Block it to add time to your week, or Dismiss.
  5. Check an email’s tone. On Email tone, paste your draft and click Check tone, or paste an email you received and click Read this email. Accept or Skip each suggestion.
  6. Reflect without AI. The Reflect tab walks you through your numbers for This week or the Last 30 days, then your wins, bottlenecks and focus for next time. Click Save review.

Good to know

  • Student names are replaced with [student] unless you tick Student names. Calendar meetings are sent as “Busy” unless you tick Calendar titles. Coach evaluation notes are only sent if you tick Sensitive HR notes (this request), which applies to one request.
  • The What’s sent tab shows the last request word for word. Preview context for this week shows what would be sent, without sending it.
  • On-device AI keeps everything on your Mac. Week planning and the review need a cloud AI key.
  • Before using a cloud provider, check that your account does not let the provider train on your data. Business or API keys usually opt out by default.

Program Assessment

Find it: Sidebar › Admin › Program Assessment (or press ⌘K and type “Program Assessment”). It only appears once switched on in Settings › School › Modules › Program Assessment (NIAAA LTC 799).

The NIAAA Quality Program Assessment (LTC 799) as a shared workspace. Rate all 10 sections, award points out of 1150 and attach evidence, then export a report for the reviewer.

Who uses it: The AD, plus colleagues you add. Read-only people can look but not change anything. It is an add-on some schools have.

Common tasks

  1. Start the assessment. The first time, follow the steps on Start your LTC 799 assessment: Connect, enter your school or department name and click Create. If your AD has invited you, click Check Again once they have added you.
  2. Work through a section. Pick a section to see its criteria and score. Click a criterion’s status to move it from Not started to In progress to Completed. Enter the points awarded and add Notes for the reviewer.
  3. Add evidence. Click Attach on a criterion, or drop files on its row. You can also paste a link (Drive, website) and press Return. Each file offers Open, Show in Finder and Remove file.
  4. Write the section narrative. Type a summary for the reviewer under Section narrative. It saves when you click away and shows who edited it last.
  5. Add colleagues. Click Members in the toolbar. Everyone you add sees the same ratings, points and files.
  6. Export the report. Click Export, then Export full report… or Export this section…. The file opens in any browser and can be printed to PDF.

Good to know

  • The total score shows at the top. If a section has more points awarded than it can count, a note says so.
  • Click Refresh or press ⌘R to get everyone’s latest changes.
  • You can keep working offline. Changes wait and send when you are back online.
  • Each person switches the module on in Settings › School on their own Mac.
